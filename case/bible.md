# Case bible: *Darkness and Distance*

A fictional true-crime podcast in eight episodes, adapted from Mary Shelley's *Frankenstein; or, The Modern
Prometheus* (1818; text of the revised 1831 edition, Project Gutenberg #84, in `source/`).

*The listener never hears this file. It exists so that every episode obeys the same rules, and so that the podcast
never contradicts the book.*

## Premise

In 2026 the journalist Tessa Lind is sent a box of photographs by a retired teacher in Kent, Judith Saville. They show
letters that came down in Judith's family: the letters Captain Robert Walton wrote to his sister, Mrs Margaret
Saville, from St Petersburg, Archangel and the Arctic ice, in a year given only as "seventeen" and a dash. Inside
them, in Walton's hand, is the statement of a dying Genevan, Victor Frankenstein, and inside that, the statement of
the being Victor says he made. Together they describe five deaths, one execution, one acquittal and a suspect who was
never charged, never caught, and last seen on an ice raft drifting north.

Tessa treats it as a cold case, and as something astonishing. Her question is "this is incredible; could it really
be true?", and her method is to lift forward, every time, what holds up: the crew's sighting, the trial record, the
witnesses, the letters Walton held. She names the doubts honestly, but the season leans toward "it seems to be true",
not toward debunking. She also asks who could have done each killing, and why the only person ever punished was a
young woman everybody now agrees was innocent.

The book's events and outcome are unchanged. William, Justine, Clerval, Elizabeth and Alphonse die as in the book;
Victor dies on Walton's ship; the creature leaves on the ice, saying he will burn himself, and is "lost in darkness
and distance". The podcast ends where the book ends: no body, no pyre found, no verdict.

## Rules of the Case

**The past (canon)**

1. **The book is the record.** Every past event, name, place, date, quote and outcome comes from the 1831 text.
   Fact IDs in `evidence.md` (E01…) are cited in the outline.
2. **Nothing is added to the past that the book does not allow.** Invented historical artefacts are allowed only
   when the book's events entail them (a register of a sentence the book describes, a second hand in a manuscript
   the book says Victor corrected). They are marked `invented-consistent` in `evidence.md`. Anything else the book
   leaves unsaid is `open`: the podcast may ask the question, never answer it.
3. **No year.** The letters give only "17—". Nobody in the podcast names a year. Months and days are used as written.

**Quotes and claims**

4. **Verbatim or nothing.** Words in quotation marks from a historical person are the book's words. Cuts are
   allowed, rewording is not. A paraphrase is said as a paraphrase ("Ernest tells him the servants found…").
5. **No inner states as fact.** The host never says what a historical person felt, knew or intended. She attributes
   it: "Victor says…", "according to the creature…". Modern guests may give opinions, labelled as opinion.
6. **Readers.** Three reader voices voice the documents: Walton (his own letters), Victor (his statement as Walton
   recorded it), the Creature (the statement Victor relays, and the cabin scene Walton witnessed). Every other
   historical quote (Justine, Elizabeth, Ernest, Kirwin, the witnesses) is read by the host inside her narration
   with a clear "she told the court". Exception: a letter Victor recites at length (Alphonse's of May 12th) is
   voiced by the Victor reader, because it reached Walton in Victor's mouth; the host says so (ep02).

**Chain of custody (the podcast's core logic)**

7. **Three layers.** (a) Walton's letters: written by Walton, dated, contemporary. (b) Victor's statement: told aloud
   to Walton over about a week from 20 August, written up by Walton each night "as nearly as possible in his own
   words". (c) The creature's statement: told to Victor on the glacier above Chamounix, remembered by Victor years
   later, relayed to Walton, written by Walton.
8. **Victor edited the record.** Walton says Victor asked to see the notes and "corrected and augmented them in many
   places, but principally in giving the life and spirit to the conversations he held with his enemy". So every
   word the creature speaks in Victor's statement has passed through the suspect's other main party. The physical
   letters show corrections in a second hand and ink (invented-consistent, E61).
9. **One layer is clean.** The last scene, in the cabin at midnight after Victor's death, Walton witnessed and wrote
   himself. The creature's words there (including "Think you that the groans of Clerval were music to my ears?")
   have no Victor in between. This is the finale's key evidence.

**Corroboration (independent of Victor)**

10. **What does not depend on Victor's word.** (a) Walton and his crew watch a gigantic figure drive a dog sledge
    north on the ice, the day before Victor is picked up (E02). (b) Walton handles the letters of Felix and Safie
    that Victor shows him (E63). (c) Walton sees and hears the creature in the cabin (E70). (d) The Geneva trial file (E25a, invented-consistent):
    Justine said steps woke her in the barn (E20), matching the creature's account of bending over her (E33).
    (e) Alibis are graded: Ireland is proved by strangers (grand jury); Ingolstadt (walking tour with Clerval, E09c)
    rests on Victor's word and the letter dates. The Irish depositions: Clerval was strangled, with finger marks, on a night when a grand jury found Victor was
    in Orkney (E44, E47). These do come through Victor, and the town is blanked in the letters (E48), but a grand
    jury of strangers accepting his alibi is a fact he had no reason to invent. (f) The seashore hamlet's report of a giant with a sledge (E68), but this one comes through
    Victor.
11. **The pattern.** William, Clerval and Elizabeth were killed by hand at the throat, each with the mark of fingers
    on the neck (E10, E44, E54). Victor has an alibi for two of the three (a walking tour near Ingolstadt for William, his word;
    in Orkney for Clerval, a grand jury). Evian is the only death where Victor was in the building and the only witness to the figure at the
    window; most of the searchers thought he had imagined it (E56).

**Discrepancies the podcast uses (never "fixes")**

12. The miniature: Justine's trial says it was found in her **pocket** (E18); the creature says he put it "in one
    of the folds of her dress" (E33).
13. Elizabeth said she put the miniature **round William's neck** an hour before he went missing (E19); the creature
    says he saw "something glittering on his breast" (E32). These agree.
14. The creature tells Walton he "strangled the innocent as they slept" (E71). Elizabeth screamed twice before she
    died (E53). Who was asleep? The podcast asks; it does not decide.
15. **How did Clerval get to Ireland?** He was last known waiting at Perth for Victor (E40). The book never says how
    he came to be on an Irish beach, or how the creature got him there. Open (E46).
16. Daniel Nugent swore the boat he saw near the body looked like the one Victor landed in (E45). The creature left
    Orkney by boat (E39). Two boats, one night, no way to tell them apart.

**The law (institutions)**

17. **Geneva** convicted Justine on circumstantial evidence plus a confession she said was extracted by her
    confessor's threats of excommunication (E21–E24). The judges' ballots were "all black". She was executed the next
    day. Her conviction is never reversed in the book: nobody ever tells a court what Victor knew. In the podcast
    the Geneva record still names her as William's murderer (invented-consistent register entry, E25).
18. **Ireland** (Mr Kirwin, magistrate): depositions, a confrontation with the body, three months' prison, the
    assizes; the grand jury "rejected the bill" on proof that Victor was in Orkney (E47).
19. **Geneva again**: a criminal judge hears Victor's full accusation after Elizabeth's death and treats it as
    delirium (E59). The creature is never formally accused anywhere else.
20. **Crimes that are not murders**: Victor's collecting from "the dissecting room and the slaughter-house" and the
    "vaults and charnel-houses" (E05), the creature setting fire to the De Lacey cottage (E30), Felix's attack on him
    and the shooting by the girl's companion (E29, E31), the theft of the hamlet's winter food and dogs (E68).

## Style

- Present-day narration, past-tense history. American host, British guests. British spelling in the scripts is
  not needed; use US spelling for the host's script throughout (one spelling for the whole season).
- Respect: William, Justine, Henry and Elizabeth get a life before their deaths. The creature gets his own account
  and his own crimes; he is neither excused nor made into a cartoon.
- Breathing room: a light moment per episode, never about a death.
- Every episode opens with a cold open (a document fragment), then "From [nothing], this is Darkness and Distance.
  I'm Tessa Lind." and a one-line recap from episode 2 on.
