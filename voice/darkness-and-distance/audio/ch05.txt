Episode 5. The Suspect's Statement.

[pause 1.0]

[[Creature]] I grasped his throat to silence him, and in a moment he lay dead at my feet.

[pause 1.5]

From a sea chest in Kent, this is Darkness and Distance. I'm Tessa Lind.

Last time, Victor Frankenstein told us what he says he did at the university of [Ingolstadt](/ˈɪŋɡəlʃtæt/): two years of stolen remains, and a November night when, he says, a being he had made opened its eyes. He ran from it. Then it was gone.

This week, it speaks.

[pause 1.5]

I need to start with a warning about what you're going to hear, because it's the most important thing in this episode.

The voice you just heard belongs to the third layer of these documents. It's what Victor says the creature told him, in a hut on a glacier, about two months after [Justine](/ʤəstˈin/)'s execution. Victor repeated it to Walton years later, on the ship. Walton wrote it down at night. And then, as Walton tells us himself, Victor read Walton's notes and "corrected and augmented them", above all in the conversations with his enemy.

I asked Judith Saville how much of this part of the manuscript is marked up.

[[Judith]] Most of it. I counted, one wet Sunday. In Robert's own letters the second ink hardly appears at all. In Victor's account of his childhood, now and then. In the parts where the creature speaks, it's on nearly every page. Sometimes a single word changed. Sometimes a whole line. And here's the frustrating thing. He didn't cross out. He scraped. You can see it against the light, where the paper's thinner. He took a penknife to Robert's ink, the way people did then, and wrote over the place. You cannot read what was there before. I've tried with a lamp and a magnifying glass and my grandson's phone. Nothing.

So the statement you're about to hear has been through the hands of the man who hates its speaker most in the world. Victor could have made the creature sound worse. He could have made him sound better. He could have put words in his mouth that explained things Victor needed explained. We can't tell. So for every claim in it, I'm going to ask the same question: can we check it against anything Victor didn't touch?

[pause 1.5]

Here's how they met, according to Victor.

It was the middle of August, nearly two months after [Justine](/ʤəstˈin/)'s death. Victor went up into the mountains above [Chamounix](/ʃæmunˈi/) to try to escape his grief, and climbed to [Montanvert](/mɑntɑnvˈɛɹ/), above the glacier called the sea of ice. He sat on a rock. And he says he saw a figure coming across the ice towards him "with superhuman speed", bounding over the crevasses he'd picked his way around.

Victor's first words to him, by his own account, were "Devil," and then, "do you dare approach me?" He threatened to trample it to dust.

The creature didn't attack. It asked to be heard. And it said something that I think is the centre of this whole case.

[[Creature]] I was benevolent and good; misery made me a fiend. Make me happy, and I shall again be virtuous.

Victor says that, partly from curiosity and partly from compassion, he agreed. He also says this, and I want you to notice it: "I had hitherto supposed him to be the murderer of my brother, and I eagerly sought a confirmation or denial of this opinion." In other words, Victor went into that hut with an open question. He came out with an answer.

They crossed the ice, climbed to a hut, and the creature lit a fire. Then it began.

[pause 1.5]

I'm going to summarise most of the statement, because it runs for a hundred pages. Here's its shape.

It begins on that November night. The creature says it remembers very little. Light. Walking. Going down. And here's a detail that fits Victor's story of the empty room: it says that before it left Victor's apartment, feeling cold, "I had covered myself with some clothes". It found its way to a forest near [Ingolstadt](/ˈɪŋɡəlʃtæt/), ate berries, drank from a brook, and wept with cold.

And the hand at the bedside, from episode four? The creature's statement doesn't mention it. It says it walked, and went down, and found itself in a forest. Two accounts of the same few hours, and they don't quite touch. Victor remembers a hand held out to him. The creature, by this account, remembers light and cold. Make of that what you will. I can't make anything of it, except that, if Victor wrote the creature's words to suit himself, he didn't use them to explain that hand.

It wandered. It found a fire someone had left and burned itself on it. It went into a village and was driven out with stones. And then it found a hovel, a lean-to built against the wall of a cottage in the countryside, and hid there.

The cottage belonged to a family named De Lacey. A blind old man, his son Felix, and his daughter Agatha. They were poor; once, the creature learned, they had been rich, in France. Through a crack in the wall, the creature watched them for a year. It learned to speak by listening to them. It learned to read when a young woman named [Safie](/sˈɑfi/) arrived, and Felix taught her the language. It secretly cut their firewood at night and left it at their door.

It also found, in the pocket of the clothes it had taken from Victor's laboratory, some papers.

[[Creature]] It was your journal of the four months that preceded my creation. You minutely described in these papers every step you took in the progress of your work; this history was mingled with accounts of domestic occurrences. You doubtless recollect these papers. Here they are.

Here they are. The creature handed Victor his own laboratory journal, in that hut. That's a document, a real physical thing, in the hands of both men. Walton never mentions seeing it. It isn't in Judith's chest. Where it went, nobody knows.

[pause 1.5]

But there's another set of papers, and these Walton did see.

[Safie](/sˈɑfi/) was the daughter of a Turkish merchant who had been condemned in Paris. Listen to this, because it's a wrongful conviction story inside our wrongful conviction story. The creature says the merchant's sentence was so unjust that "all Paris was indignant", and Felix helped him escape. For that, the De Lacey family lost everything and were exiled. [Safie](/sˈɑfi/) wrote letters to Felix. And the creature says:

[[Creature]] I have copies of these letters, for I found means, during my residence in the hovel, to procure the implements of writing; and the letters were often in the hands of Felix or Agatha. Before I depart I will give them to you; they will prove the truth of my tale.

So the creature handed over evidence. Two sets of papers. And we know what happened to the second set, because in his own letter of the twenty-sixth of August, Walton writes this:

[[Walton]] His tale is connected and told with an appearance of the simplest truth, yet I own to you that the letters of Felix and [Safie](/sˈɑfi/), which he showed me, and the apparition of the monster seen from our ship, brought to me a greater conviction of the truth of his narrative than his asseverations, however earnest and connected.

That's Walton's own voice. Not Victor's statement, not the creature's. Walton held those letters. He saw the giant on the ice. And those two things, more than anything Victor said, are what made him believe.

The letters of Felix and [Safie](/sˈɑfi/) are not in Judith's chest either.

[[Judith]] I'd give a great deal for them. I've been through every fold of that oilcloth.

[pause 1.5]

Now the part of the statement that matters for our case.

The creature says that after a year of watching, it decided to reveal itself. It waited until the young people had gone out, and went in to the blind father, who couldn't see it, and they talked. And the old man was kind. Then the others came home.

Agatha fainted. [Safie](/sˈɑfi/) ran out. And Felix, the creature says, "dashed me to the ground and struck me violently with a stick". The creature says it could have torn him apart, and didn't. It ran.

The family left. They never came back. And one night, in a high wind, the creature set fire to the cottage and danced around it while it burned.

Then it went looking for Victor. Victor's papers had named Geneva as his home town. It travelled towards Geneva, by night, through autumn and winter. In the spring, it says, it pulled a girl out of a fast river after she slipped and fell in. The man who was with her tore her out of its arms, ran, and when it followed, shot it. "The ball had entered my shoulder." It spent weeks in the woods healing. It says this is where it swore revenge on all mankind.

So, by the creature's account, before anyone in this story died, it had been beaten, stoned and shot. Those are crimes too. Nobody was ever tried for those.

[pause 1.5]

It reached the fields outside Geneva in the evening. It lay down in a hiding place and slept. And it was woken by a child.

[[Creature]] At this time a slight sleep relieved me from the pain of reflection, which was disturbed by the approach of a beautiful child, who came running into the recess I had chosen, with all the sportiveness of infancy.

The creature says its first idea was not to kill him. It thought a child would be too young to be horrified by its face, and that it could take him and raise him as a companion. So it grabbed him. The boy screamed and covered his eyes, and called it a monster and an ogre, and threatened it with his father. And then he said his father's name.

[[Creature]] Hideous monster! Let me go. My papa is a syndic—he is Monsieur Frankenstein—he will punish you. You dare not keep me.

That's William. You remember from episode two that his father was a former syndic. And it's exactly the kind of thing a frightened small boy says.

[[Creature]] Frankenstein! you belong then to my enemy—to him towards whom I have sworn eternal revenge; you shall be my first victim.

[[Creature]] The child still struggled and loaded me with epithets which carried despair to my heart; I grasped his throat to silence him, and in a moment he lay dead at my feet.

I'm not going to linger on it. Dr [Okafor](/Okˈɑfɔɹ/) told us in episode two that with a small child it doesn't take long, and that the marks would be consistent with a hand at the throat. This statement says a hand at the throat.

Then the creature says it saw "something glittering on his breast". A portrait of a beautiful woman. Remember, Elizabeth said she'd put it round William's neck. It took it.

[pause 1.5]

And now the barn. Listen carefully.

[[Creature]] While I was overcome by these feelings, I left the spot where I had committed the murder, and seeking a more secluded hiding-place, I entered a barn which had appeared to me to be empty. A woman was sleeping on some straw; she was young, not indeed so beautiful as her whose portrait I held, but of an agreeable aspect and blooming in the loveliness of youth and health.

The creature says it bent over her and whispered to her. That she stirred. That it was suddenly afraid she'd wake, see it and denounce it. And it decided she would pay instead.

[[Creature]] I bent over her and placed the portrait securely in one of the folds of her dress. She moved again, and I fled.

That's [Justine](/ʤəstˈin/). In the barn belonging to a cottage, where she'd taken shelter when the gates of Geneva were shut.

Now remember what we said at the start. This passage has been through Victor's hands. It is marked up like the rest of the creature's speech. On its own, it could be Victor writing the explanation he needed for the jewel in [Justine](/ʤəstˈin/)'s pocket.

But it isn't on its own. Dr [Reber](/ɹˈAbəɹ/), in episode three, read us the clerk's note from [Justine](/ʤəstˈin/)'s trial file. She slept in a barn. She was woken by steps. She could not account for the portrait. Steps.

[[[Reber](/ɹˈAbəɹ/)]] I have read that note many times now with the statement beside it. The clerk wrote it down in court, in Geneva, before the glacier, before Victor had told anyone his story. On the documents, [Justine](/ʤəstˈin/) said someone came into that barn. The court did not ask who.

But I have to put the obvious objection, because Dr [Reber](/ɹˈAbəɹ/) put it to me first. Victor was in that court. He heard [Justine](/ʤəstˈin/) say "some steps disturbed her". Months or years later, he had Walton's pages in front of him and a penknife in his hand. If he wanted a barn scene that fitted her testimony, he knew exactly what to write.

[[[Reber](/ɹˈAbəɹ/)]] Yes. So I asked myself: if I were Victor, inventing this, what would I write? I would write the steps. I would make the creature walk in at dawn and stand over her as she woke. He does not. In the statement there are no steps at all. The creature finds her asleep, it bends over her, it whispers, she stirs, it puts the portrait in her dress and runs.

And the time is wrong for a forger. The creature goes to the barn soon after the killing, in the evening, it seems. [Justine](/ʤəstˈin/) says she only got there hours later, after searching, and was woken towards morning. A man making the story fit would have made it fit better.

So here's where I come down. The trial file proves that [Justine](/ʤəstˈin/) said someone was there. It can't prove who. The creature's statement says it was the creature, in a version that matches her in one detail and misses her in two. It's not proof. It's the kind of untidy agreement you get from two people describing the same night from different ends of it.

And there's the other thing that doesn't line up. The servant found the picture in [Justine](/ʤəstˈin/)'s pocket. The creature says it put it in a fold of her dress. Is that a contradiction? A fold of a dress near a pocket, a sleeper who turns over? I don't know. But I promised to tell you every place the accounts disagree, and with the timing, that's two.

[pause 1.5]

After William, the creature says, it haunted the area for some days, then went up into the mountains. And there, in the hut, it got to the point. It wanted Victor to make it a companion. A female, "of the same species", who would not be horrified by it. If he did, it said, they would leave Europe and go "to the vast wilds of South America", and no human would ever see them again.

Victor refused. Then he wavered. And at the end of the afternoon, he said this:

[[Victor]] I consent to your demand, on your solemn oath to quit Europe for ever, and every other place in the neighbourhood of man, as soon as I shall deliver into your hands a female who will accompany you in your exile.

The creature said, "I shall watch their progress", meaning Victor's labours, and went off down the mountain.

So here's where we are. A child is dead, an innocent woman has been executed, and the man who knows why has just agreed to make a second one.
