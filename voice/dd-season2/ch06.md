# Episode 6: The Window

[grave] [[Creature]] It is well. I go; but remember, I shall be with you on your wedding-night.

Those are the last words Victor Frankenstein says he heard from the creature on an island in the Orkneys, at night, with nobody else in the house. Then he says it ran down to the shore, and got into a boat, and was gone.

---

From a sea chest in Kent, this is Darkness and Distance, season two. I'm Tessa Lind.

Last time, the glacier, and the creature's own statement. Its confession to William's murder, and its account of the barn, where it says it slipped the portrait into Justine's dress. Nearly every detail was in the trial Victor sat through. And the conservator found Victor's second ink heaviest of all in the creature's speeches. But there's also a bundle of letters Victor could not have written, which Walton held in his own hands.

This week, the theory meets the hardest thing in the whole record. I'll get there. First, the island.

---

Henry Clerval wanted to go to India. That's the first thing to know about him, if all you know so far is that he nursed his friend through a winter. Henry was a merchant's son from Geneva who studied Persian, Arabic and Sanskrit. He loved poetry. He made up stories in the style of the old Persian writers. And he planned to go to India one day, believing his languages would let him play a part in its trade.

When Victor said he needed to travel to England, Henry came along. Victor says that the whole journey, through London and Oxford, through the Lakes and up into Scotland, Henry was delighted with everything, and that he, Victor, could hardly bear it, because he knew what he was going to do at the end of it.

In Scotland, Victor left Henry at Perth. He said he wanted to travel alone for a while. Henry agreed to wait for him there.

Victor went north, to "the remotest of the Orkneys". He describes it as hardly more than a rock, beaten by the waves. A few miserable cows. Three huts. Five people. One of the huts was empty, and he rented it.

I went to the Orkney archive in Kirkwall, because if five people on a rock in the sea had a stranger living among them for months, somebody might have written it down. The church kept the island's affairs in a book, the minutes of the kirk session. The archivist knew exactly which parish to look in. She found it in an afternoon.

[[Archive]] A stranger from abroad has the empty hut these several months. Seen about it by the people of the island. Departed by boat.

No name. No date that I'm allowed to say. But a stranger from abroad, in the empty hut, for months, seen by the people of the island. Remember that line about being seen. We're going to need it.

---

So. Victor is on an island, alone, with a laboratory. And on the night he describes, he's at work on the second creature, the female, and he stops. He starts to think about what he's doing. What if she's worse than the first? What if they have children?

[grave] [[Victor]] I trembled and my heart failed within me, when, on looking up, I saw by the light of the moon the daemon at the casement. A ghastly grin wrinkled his lips as he gazed on me, where I sat fulfilling the task which he had allotted to me.

Victor tears the half-made thing to pieces, in front of the face at the window. The face howls, and goes.

Hours pass. Victor sits by the window, looking at a calm sea. And then he hears oars. Somebody lands near the house. The door creaks. He wants to run to his neighbours for help, but he can't move. And here's how he describes that feeling.

[[Victor]] I was overcome by the sensation of helplessness, so often felt in frightful dreams, when you in vain endeavour to fly from an impending danger, and was rooted to the spot.

Footsteps in the passage. The door opens. The creature is in the room, and they argue. The creature says it followed him all the way from Switzerland, along the Rhine, through England and Scotland. Victor says he will never make another. And the creature says the words I opened with. I shall be with you on your wedding-night.

Victor tries to grab it. It runs, and he sees it in a boat, shooting across the water.

Then he's alone. And he says something I'd like you to hear exactly.

[[Victor]] I walked up and down my room hastily and perturbed, while my imagination conjured up a thousand images to torment and sting me.

[grave] He thinks about the threat. With you on your wedding night. And he decides what it means. That's when the creature will kill him, Victor. That's the hour fixed for his own death. He's not afraid of it, he says. He only weeps for Elizabeth, the girl he's to marry, and for how she'll grieve.

---

I played the island to Dr Kessler. Then I asked her what she'd mark in the margin.

[[Kessler]] Every single thing. Look at it. A man alone, at night, doing the work that broke him once before. He looks up and sees a face at the window. Nobody else on the island is awake. Then he describes being frozen, and he tells us himself what it's like: it's like a frightful dream. Then the creature comes in and speaks his own worst fear aloud. And when it's gone, his imagination conjures a thousand images. If you'd asked me to write a scene that, on this theory, shows a man arguing with himself, I'd have written this one.

The next day a letter comes from Henry, asking Victor to join him at Perth. The morning after that, Victor packs up his instruments. The remains of the half-made female are on the floor. He puts them in a basket with stones, and between two and three in the morning, he rows out about four miles and drops them into the sea. Then he falls asleep in the boat. The wind gets up. He drifts all the next day, lost. At last he sights land, and puts in at a small harbour town.

It's Ireland. And a crowd is waiting.

---

They take him to the magistrate, Mr Kirwin, an old man, Victor says, calm and kind. And the witnesses give their evidence. These depositions come to us through Victor's statement, but they are other people's words, given under oath, in front of a magistrate.

A fisherman, out the night before with his son and his brother-in-law, Daniel Nugent. About ten o'clock, a strong north wind, so they came in to a creek two miles below the harbour. Walking up the sand in the dark, the fisherman tripped over something. It was the body of a man.

They thought he'd drowned. But his clothes weren't wet. And the body wasn't cold yet. A handsome young man, they said, about twenty-five.

[grave] [[Victor]] He had apparently been strangled, for there was no sign of any violence except the black mark of fingers on his neck.

Daniel Nugent swore that just before his brother-in-law fell, he'd seen a boat, with a single man in it, close to the shore. As far as he could tell by starlight, it was the same boat Victor had landed in. And a woman who lived by the beach said that about an hour before she heard of the body, she saw a boat with one man in it push off from that part of the shore.

Kirwin has Victor taken to see the body. And Victor sees who it is.

[grave] It's Henry. Henry Clerval, who had been waiting for him in Perth.

[grave] [[Victor]] Have my murderous machinations deprived you also, my dearest Henry, of life? Two I have already destroyed; other victims await their destiny; but you, Clerval, my friend, my benefactor.

He's carried out in convulsions. And then comes a fever that lasts two months.

---

Here's why I believe the theory has something real here, and it's in Victor's own words about those two months.

[grave] [[Victor]] My ravings, as I afterwards heard, were frightful; I called myself the murderer of William, of Justine, and of Clerval.

He says that sometimes he begged the people around him to help him destroy the fiend that was tormenting him. And sometimes he felt the monster's fingers on his own throat, and screamed. Only Kirwin understood him, because he was raving in his own language.

Later, when his father comes to take him home, Victor says it to his face. "William, Justine, and Henry, they all died by my hands." His father, he says, sometimes seemed to want to understand, and sometimes took it as the leftovers of delirium.

Over Henry's body, in front of a magistrate. In two months of raving. To his own father. Three times, to three audiences, Victor says he killed them. Every time, by his own account, the people around him take it for the fever talking.

[[Kessler]] And every time, he explains it as a figure of speech. My machinations. In effect, not in deed. What I'd want to know, if this were my case, is what he felt when he said it. We can't know that. But a man who keeps confessing, and keeps being told he's ill, is a man the system around him has decided not to hear. That cuts both ways, of course.

---

Now. Here's the thing that nags me, and it isn't a nag this time. It's a wall.

[[Reber]] The Irish court did not let Victor Frankenstein go because it liked him. It sent the matter to a grand jury, at the assizes, a hundred miles away. And the grand jury rejected the bill. The reason is in Victor's own statement, and it is a very precise reason. It was proved that he was on the Orkney Islands at the hour the body of his friend was found.

I asked Reber what proved means, in a court like that.

[[Reber]] Witnesses. Mr Kirwin collected them. On the documents, the people who could say where he was are the people of that island. He says himself that he had been talking with several of them around that time. Your kirk session minute cannot say where he was that night. But it confirms he lived there for months, among people who knew him by sight.

Then I called Dr Okafor and gave him the timeline.

[[Okafor]] Found about ten at night. Clothes dry. Body not yet cold. That's a death within an hour or two. Maybe a little more on a cold beach, not much. At that hour, by his own account and the court's, Victor is on an island in the Orkneys, hundreds of miles away by sea. He doesn't sail until two or three in the morning. He doesn't land in Ireland until the next evening. Whatever the theory is, Victor's hands are not on Henry Clerval's throat at ten o'clock that night. The sea's too wide.

[grave] So there it is. On the documents, Victor Frankenstein could not have killed Henry Clerval. Someone else did. Perhaps someone with a boat, seen by Daniel Nugent in the starlight, seen by a woman at her door. Strong enough to strangle a grown man with one pair of hands.

And whoever it was had a head start. The thing Victor says he saw on the island left in its boat two nights before. How Henry got from Perth to that beach, nobody ever says. That's open, and it stays open.

I put that to Dr Kessler. I half expected her to drop the whole theory. She didn't, but she didn't dodge it either.

[[Kessler]] Then on this theory, Victor wasn't alone. Somebody else was in it, at least for Henry. I can't tell you who, and nothing in the documents names anyone. Or there was no such somebody, and the being Victor described was real. Those are the two doors. I can't close either of them. What I'd say is that Henry's death doesn't explain away the fever, or the face at the window, or the ravings. It just means that, whatever Victor was, he wasn't the whole of it.

Victor's own words come back to me, over the body. My murderous machinations. Two I have already destroyed. I'll leave them there.

---

Victor's father came to Ireland to fetch him. Soon after he was freed, they sailed for home. Victor took laudanum every night to sleep.

On the way, in Paris, a letter reached him from Elizabeth. It was about their marriage.

[grave] And all the way home, Victor says, he kept thinking about the words on the island. He was quite sure what they meant. That on his wedding night, the creature would come for him.
